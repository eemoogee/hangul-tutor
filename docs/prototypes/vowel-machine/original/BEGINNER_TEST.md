# Beginner observation · 3–5 people

Use complete beginners who have not studied Hangul. Test locally from `index.html` on a phone if possible. No sign-in, audio, or personal data is required. Ask permission to observe and take notes; do not record faces, voice, or names by default.

1. Alternate starting method: learner 1 uses Move strokes first, learner 2 uses Three keys first, and so on. Point out only the method tabs and say, “Explore until you feel ready to make the shapes again.” Avoid teaching the four answers.
2. Let the learner complete Explore, Prove, and Read the imprint for the first method. If stuck, let them choose the built-in clue. Note spontaneous comments, mistaken constructions, and whether the cue labels make sense.
3. Let them use the other method. Ask, “What changed when you made the same vowels this way?” Do not tell them which method should be better. Record which method was first; report first-method and second-method observations separately.
4. Ask them to explain or sketch why ㅏ differs from ㅓ and why ㅗ differs from ㅜ. This is a conversational probe, separate from the app's immediate check.
5. Record per method: completed or stopped, first unaided Prove submissions correct out of four, attempts and optional-clue use by target, four immediate vowel-recognition responses and their tested order, confusing controls or text, and preferred method with reason. Note later correct answers after component feedback separately from first unaided success. Do not compare raw taps. The in-memory `window.__vowelMachine.methodReport('stroke'|'keys')` is available to a reviewer before the page closes.

Look for a pattern across participants, not a score. A promising signal would be learners explaining the position rule in their own words and recognizing the four vowels without the rail or keypad. This immediate multiple-choice check does not establish independent syllable reading, long-term recall, or pronunciation.
